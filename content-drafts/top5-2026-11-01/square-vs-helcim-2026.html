Helcim costs less than Square at every volume we priced, in person and online. How we priced Helcim against Square

The two providers price cards in different ways, so a side-by-side rate list misleads. Square charges one flat rate per sale that already contains the card network's cost. Helcim charges the network's cost at whatever it is for that card, then adds its own published margin. To compare them on the same terms we turn both into an effective rate: the total fee on a month of sales divided by the volume.

That needs three assumptions, and we print all three. A $50 average sale, because the fixed cents per transaction matter more on small tickets. A card mix of 60% credit and 40% debit. And an estimate of wholesale interchange plus network assessments for that mix: 1.55% in person and 1.70% online. The debit part is anchored by the Federal Reserve's Regulation II cap of 21¢ + 0.05% + 1¢ for large issuers; the credit part uses typical Visa and Mastercard consumer credit interchange. It is an estimate, and your own statement will show the real number.

Software, hardware, chargebacks and optional services are priced separately below, because they depend on how you run the business rather than on the card.

Square: simple pricing, and a plan choice that matters

Square's appeal is that you can sign up, plug in a reader and sell the same afternoon, with a free POS app and deposits the next business day at no cost. The price for that simplicity is a flat rate with a 15¢ fixed fee on every in-person sale. On Square Free that is 2.6% + 15¢, which works out to 2.90% on a $50 sale and much more on small tickets: an $8 coffee pays 4.48%.

The paid plans lower the percentage, but they cost money every month, so they only pay off above a certain volume. Each step down is 0.1% of your in-person sales, so the arithmetic is quick:

  • Plus ($49 a month) beats Free once you take more than about $49,000 a month in person.
  • Premium ($149 a month) beats Plus above about $100,000 a month, and beats Free above about $74,500.
  • Both plans also bring the online store and invoice rate down from 3.3% + 30¢ to 2.9% + 30¢, which can change the math if you sell a lot online.

Read which Square product takes each payment. The Online API rate of 2.9% + 30¢ is available on every plan, but Square Free's own online store and invoices cost 3.3% + 30¢. International cards add 1.5%. Square's ACH bank payments cost 1% with a $1 minimum, capped at $10 on Plus and Premium invoices and $5 through the API. Square offers custom pricing only above $250,000 a year in card sales.

Who it fits: new and small in-person sellers who want everything in one box and value next-day deposits more than the last few tenths of a percent. If you are choosing between the two big flat-rate brands, our Square vs Stripe fees comparison shows where Stripe's 2.7% + 5¢ comes out ahead.

Helcim: pay the card's real cost, plus a small margin

Helcim passes the card network's interchange through at cost and adds a margin it publishes for US merchants. Under $50,000 a month that margin is 0.40% + 8¢ in person and 0.50% + 25¢ for online and keyed sales. It steps down on its own as volume grows, with no negotiation:

Helcim's published volume discounts use your average processing volume over the preceding three months, so a single big month does not move you down a tier. There is no monthly fee, no setup fee, no PCI fee and no cancellation fee. Deposits arrive the next business morning at no cost; Helcim publishes no instant option.

The trade-off is predictability. Because you pay each card's own interchange, a month heavy in premium rewards or corporate cards costs more than a month of debit cards, even at the same volume. Check the add-ons too: recurring payments add 0.4% per transaction, Tap to Pay on iPhone adds 10¢, and a chargeback costs $15 if you lose it and $0 if you win. ACH costs 0.5% + 25¢, capped at $6.

Who it fits: small and mid-size US merchants who want the lowest published cost without a contract and are comfortable with a rate that moves a little with the card mix. Online sellers can see the same comparison against a flat rate in Helcim vs Stripe.

Worked example: $25,000 across 500 in-person sales

Take one location with 500 card sales a month averaging $50. On Square Free the fee is simple: $25,000 × 2.6% plus 500 × 15¢, or $725. On Helcim you pay the wholesale card cost plus Helcim's margin, which at this tier is $25,000 × 0.40% plus 500 × 8¢, or $140.

Hardware, software and payouts

This is where Square earns its higher rate. Its POS app is free on the Free plan, its hardware starts at $59, and the system covers inventory, staff and reports in one place. Helcim's Card Reader costs $199 and its Smart Terminal $349, or $32 a month for a year, with optional 4G data at $7 a month. If you are opening one small location on a tight budget, the hardware difference alone covers a few months of the fee gap.

Payouts are close. Square deposits the next business day for free and sells instant or same-day transfers at 1.95% of the amount moved. Helcim deposits the next business morning for free and offers no paid instant option. If you often need money the same day, Square has the feature and Helcim does not; if next morning is enough, both are free. More options are in our guide to same-day and next-day deposit processors.

Contracts and lock-in

Neither provider locks you into a contract. Square says there are no locked-in contracts and you can cancel or switch plans at any time. Helcim lists $0 setup and $0 cancellation fees. That makes this one of the lower-risk switches in payments: you can open a Helcim account, run some sales through it for a month and compare the statement with Square's before moving everything.

The real switching cost is equipment and workflow. Square hardware works with Square, and Helcim's readers work with Helcim, so moving means buying new readers. If you rely on Square's POS for inventory or staff, check that whatever you move to does the same job. Fees to check before you switch

The card rate is most of the bill, but not all of it. Price these lines for the way you actually sell:

  • Recurring billing: Helcim adds 0.4% per recurring transaction. Square's recurring invoices follow its online and invoice rates for your plan.
  • Keyed sales: Square charges 3.5% + 15¢ for manually entered cards and card on file; Helcim charges its online margin on top of interchange, which comes to about 2.70% on a $50 sale under $50K a month.
  • Chargebacks: Helcim charges $15 only when you lose a dispute. Square does not list a chargeback fee on the pricing pages we checked; confirm it in your agreement.
  • Bank payments: Helcim ACH is 0.5% + 25¢, capped at $6. Square ACH is 1% with a $1 minimum.
  • International cards: Square adds 1.5%. On Helcim the higher interchange of a foreign card is passed through.

Verdict: Helcim for cost, Square for convenience

Pick Helcim if the processing bill is the number you want to shrink. On our method it is cheaper than Square at $10,000, $50,000 and $250,000 a month, in person and online, by about $79, $430 and $2,050 a month in person. It has no monthly fee and no contract, and its margin keeps falling as you grow.

Pick Square if you are just starting, want a free POS and cheap hardware in one box, or need same-day transfers. It also wins on cost if your customers pay mostly with premium rewards cards, because a flat rate absorbs that cost for you.
